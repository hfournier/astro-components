- **Role and name:** it's a `<div>` with `role="toolbar"`, as M3 asks on the web, so assistive technology announces its controls as one set. Give it a name with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`; a toolbar without one fails the build. A vertical toolbar has `aria-orientation="vertical"`, so screen readers know which arrow keys move in it.
- **Keyboard:** the toolbar itself isn't focusable. Tab lands on its first control and moves through the rest, and the arrow keys move between them too: left and right in a horizontal toolbar, mirrored right to left, and up and down in a vertical one. The arrow keys stop at the ends and skip disabled controls. Space or Enter activates the focused one. Each shows a focus ring.
- **Unlike the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern:** its controls are each a tab stop, rather than the toolbar being one tab stop with the arrow keys moving inside it. M3's Accessibility page has Tab move through all of a toolbar's actions (ADR-0036).
- **The FAB:** a Fab in the `fab` slot is its own control beside the toolbar, outside its `toolbar` role, as M3 asks on the web. Tab reaches it after the toolbar's controls; the toolbar's arrow keys don't.
- **Controls that use the arrow keys:** in a text field, the arrows move the caret, so Tab moves on from it. A ButtonGroup or ChipGroup inside moves between its own items first, and the arrows leave it at its ends. A menu opened from the toolbar keeps its own keys.
- **Names:** name each icon button after what it does with its `label`, such as "Share" or "Delete". The label is also its tooltip. A toggle button announces whether it's selected through `aria-pressed`, and shows its filled icon when selected, so its state doesn't rest on color alone.
- **Targets:** each control has at least a 48px target, and the toolbar spaces its items by their targets, so they never overlap.
- **Reachable while scrolling:** a toolbar that leaves on scroll (`scrollBehavior="exit-always"`) stays in the tab order. When one of its controls takes focus, it comes back, and it stays while focus is in it, so keyboard and screen reader users can always reach its actions. Its motion follows reduced motion.
- **Right to left:** a horizontal toolbar mirrors, its first control at the right and its FAB at the left. If the order of the actions matters, such as Next at the trailing edge, it stays at the trailing edge, now at the left.
