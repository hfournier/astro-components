## Attributes

Name the carousel with `aria-label`, or `aria-labelledby` pointing at a visible heading, after what its items are, such as "Trip photos". A carousel without either fails the build. `class` and every other attribute go on its `<div>`. Its height is 221px, which gives its items 205px, as in Compose's sample; set another with a class such as `h-80`. A full-screen carousel is the window's height (`h-dvh`); give it the height of the page's content area with a class, under a top app bar for instance.

## Layout

Every item is laid out at the large size and scrolls natively, and a script, Compose's own layout ported to the web, sizes each item for where it is in the row. Each item grows or shrinks as it moves between its places, and snaps into place when the row stops. Its visual stays at the large size, so the item's edges move over it: M3's parallax.

- **Multi-browse:** the large items, then a medium one, then a small one, 40 to 56px wide. There's always at least one of each, as M3's Specs ask: in a 412px carousel, one large item, a medium one and a small one; a wider one fits more large items.
- **Hero:** one large item, then a small one, 40 to 56px wide, that previews the next. A wider carousel with a `preferredItemWidth` fits more large items.
- **Center-aligned hero:** one large item, centered, between two small ones. At the row's start, the first item is large at the start, with the next two small after it; at its end, the last item is large at the end. With fewer than three items, it's start-aligned, as a hero carousel.
- **Uncontained:** every item at one width, `preferredItemWidth`, as many as fit and the next cut off by the carousel's edge. The items don't change size, so they can hold more text, or other content below the visual.
- **Uncontained multi-aspect ratio:** as uncontained, but each item as wide as its CarouselItem `aspectRatio` makes it at the carousel's height, from 9:16 to 16:9. Use it only for items of different widths.
- **Full-screen:** one item, filling the carousel edge to edge, and the next 16px below it. As it scrolls, the item leaving shrinks over its visual. Use it in portrait only, at compact and medium window sizes, for content taller than it is wide.
- **Spacing:** 8px between items and above and below them (16px between full-screen items, with none around them). The row is padded 16px at the start before it scrolls, and, but for the uncontained layouts, at the end once it's scrolled to the end; in between, items reach its edges.

Give the carousel the full width of its column, and at least room for one large and one small item.

## Scrolling

A multi-browse carousel scrolls through many items at once: a long swipe carries it on, and it snaps where it stops. A hero carousel stops at every item, so a swipe moves it one item, as M3's Overview asks. The uncontained layouts scroll freely and stop wherever they're let go, as M3 recommends for them. A full-screen carousel scrolls vertically and stops at every item, which always fills it.

## Show all

On a page that scrolls vertically, M3 asks for a way to see every item without scrolling the carousel sideways; a full-screen carousel doesn't need one. Put a **Show all** button below the carousel, or an arrow icon button beside its heading, that opens a page listing every item. Don't put buttons inside the carousel, beside it or over it.

## Motion

The items change size and place as the row scrolls, under the pointer or the finger, so they move at the scroll's own speed. With reduced motion, there's no parallax and the items don't change size: every one is the same size, and the row scrolls to its edges, padded 16px at its ends. A multi-browse carousel's items are all at the preferred width, and an uncontained one's stay as they are. A hero carousel's are at its large size, so the next item shows in part, as M3's Accessibility page asks; a center-aligned one snaps each to the center. An item that takes focus or is clicked while it's small scrolls into place, large, and does so at once with reduced motion.
