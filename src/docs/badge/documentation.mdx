## When to use it

As in M3, a badge belongs on a navigation destination, such as an item in a NavBar or NavRail, an app bar's action or a Tab, to show a notification, a count or a status:

- **A small dot** for an unread notification without a count.
- **A large badge** for a count or a short status, up to 4 characters.

When a badge marks unread notifications, hide it once its destination is selected, by no longer passing a `label` (or rendering the Badge) on that page.

## Placement

- **Where space is tight,** such as an app bar, use a dot, which stays inside the icon's corner, so it can't run into the edge of the screen.
- **Where there's room,** such as a navigation rail, a large badge won't collide with anything.
- **When text or another element follows the icon,** don't let a large badge overlap it: place it after the text with `placement="inline"`, or use a dot. NavBarItem, NavRailItem and Tab already do this.
- **Keep it in its place:** don't move a badge from the icon's top-end corner, or place it over the middle of the icon.

In a right-to-left page, an anchored badge moves to the icon's top-left corner, its trailing corner there, with M3's offsets mirrored. An inline badge follows the text, so it comes after the label either way.

## Color

Keep the default `error` and `on-error` color roles. If you override them with a class, the badge still needs a contrast of at least 3:1 against whatever it sits on.

## Anchoring

By default the badge is `position: fixed`, anchored to the element it decorates, so it can go anywhere in the markup and isn't clipped by that element's `overflow`. With `placement="inline"` it's an ordinary inline element instead, and needs no anchor.

The badge follows the anchor named in its `--ac-anchorname-badge` custom property. Give the element it decorates a matching `anchor-name`. Anchor names are dashed idents, and each must be unique on the page:

```astro
<Icon class="[anchor-name:--inbox-badge]" name="inbox" label="Inbox, 3 unread" />
<Badge class="[--ac-anchorname-badge:--inbox-badge]" label={3} />
```

## Accessibility

A badge is visual only: it's always rendered with `aria-hidden="true"`, since screen readers would otherwise read its count on its own ("3") with no context. Put the full meaning in the decorated element's accessible name instead, after its own name, as M3 does: a count, such as Icon's `label="Inbox, 3 unread"`, and keep the two in sync whenever the count changes.

A dot needs words too, or a screen-reader user never learns there's anything new: M3 announces it as "New notification", e.g. `label="Inbox, new notification"`. NavBarItem, NavRailItem and Tab add a count or "New notification" to their names for you, and their `badgeDescription` prop changes the phrase.

Use Icon's `label` prop rather than a raw `aria-label`: without `label`, Icon adds `aria-hidden="true"`, which hides the `aria-label` too.
