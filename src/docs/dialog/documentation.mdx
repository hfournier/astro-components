## When to use it

Dialogs interrupt, so use them sparingly: for critical information, or a decision that has to be made before the task can go on. For low- and medium-priority messages, show something in the page's flow instead, or a snackbar.

## Headline

Make the headline a brief, clear statement or question, such as "Discard draft?". It can wrap to a second line. Don't apologize ("Sorry for the interruption"), raise an alarm ("Warning!") or ask a vague question ("Are you sure?").

## Actions

Give a dialog at most two actions:

- A single action must be an acknowledgement. Use DialogAcknowledge.
- Two actions are a dismissing action and a confirming one, with the confirming action closest to the edge. Use DialogConfirm.
- Don't add a third action such as "Learn more", which takes people away from the task. Give that information before the dialog opens, or in its content.
- If the confirming action needs a choice first, such as a selected option, disable it until one is made. Never disable the dismissing action. DialogConfirm's actions can't be disabled, so build such a dialog on Dialog, with your own Buttons in its `actions` slot.

When two labels are too long to fit side by side, the actions stack, with the confirming action above the dismissing one, both at the end.

## Roles

On the web, M3 gives alert and confirmation dialogs `role="alertdialog"`, which DialogConfirm and DialogAcknowledge set. A plain Dialog keeps the native `dialog` role, for forms and other content. For an alert built on Dialog, pass `role="alertdialog"` and `aria-describedby="<id>-content"`, so its content is read along with its headline.

## Position

A dialog is centered in the window. On large windows, M3 allows another position, such as next to what opened it. Use `overlayClass` for that, keeping the dialog at least 56px from the window's edges, and never partly off screen.

## Initial focus

When the dialog opens, focus goes to its first interactive element, or to the one with `autofocus`. DialogConfirm deliberately focuses its dismissing action rather than the first one, so a reflexive Enter never confirms.

## Scrolling

Only the content scrolls, with the headline and actions kept in view, and a divider shows at whichever edge has content scrolled out of view. Where you can, keep the content short enough not to scroll.
