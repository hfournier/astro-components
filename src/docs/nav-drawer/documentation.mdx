## M3 Expressive

M3 no longer recommends the navigation drawer: it suggests an expanded navigation rail instead, which does most of what a drawer does and adapts better across window sizes. Use a NavRail with `isExpandable` for that. NavDrawer still makes sense beside a collapsed rail whose destinations have second-level destinations of their own: the rail stays compact, and the drawer shows one destination's sub-items at a time.

## Standard and modal

From the large breakpoint (1200px) up, the drawer is M3's standard drawer: always visible, in the layout, on `surface` at elevation 0, with an `outline-variant` border on its start side that separates it from a rail on `surface`. Below large, it's M3's modal drawer, or a drawer that isn't modal with `isModal={false}`: an overlay on `surface-container-low` at elevation 1. A drawer with its own `bgColor` keeps it in both.

## Layout

Put the NavDrawer right after its NavRail (with `isExpandable={false}`), as siblings. From the large breakpoint up, the drawer takes its place in the layout, so give it a column, for example with a grid of `grid-cols-1 medium:grid-cols-[auto_1fr] large:grid-cols-[auto_auto_1fr]` on their parent. Below large it's a fixed overlay stacked under the rail, so it takes no space and slides out from under the rail's end edge. It's on the start side of the screen, as M3 asks: right of the rail, or left of it in a right-to-left page, with its rounded corners and border following. Below medium the drawer isn't used: the rail is hidden until its MenuToggle opens it as a modal expanded rail (see NavRail), and the drawer moves its sections into the rail, after its destinations, where only the current page's section shows. From medium up they move back. It finds its rail and rail items by itself, matching each rail item's `href` to a section's `parentHref`, so there's nothing to wire up.

The drawer slides out from under the rail, so NavRail is `z-30`, the drawer `z-20` and its scrim `z-10`. Anything else on the page with a higher z-index shows above the open drawer.
