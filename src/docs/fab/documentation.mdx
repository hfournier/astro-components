## When to use it

Use a FAB for a screen's primary, constructive action, such as create, compose, favorite, share or starting a process. Don't use one for minor, overflow or destructive actions, such as archive, delete or alerts. Give it a clear, filled icon, and a `label` that names the action, such as `"Compose a new message"`.

## Placement

Use one FAB per screen, for its single most important action. A FAB isn't positioned on its own: it sits wherever you put it, such as in a NavRail's `fab` slot or fixed to a corner of the page (e.g. `class="fixed end-4 bottom-4"`; `end-*` keeps it in the trailing corner right to left). Use an extended FAB where a label makes the action clearer and there's room for it.

- **Compact and medium windows:** the lower trailing corner.
- **Expanded and larger windows:** the upper leading region, such as a NavRail's `fab` slot. Screen reader and keyboard users also reach it earlier there.

A fixed FAB can partly cover the content it floats over, but it must never completely hide another control's focus indicator (WCAG 2.4.11). Leave room for it, such as with bottom padding on the page's content.

## Sizes

M3 recommends the medium FAB (`size="md"`) for most windows. Use the large FAB (`size="lg"`) where the action needs more prominence, or on expanded and larger windows. The default FAB suits compact windows with other actions on screen.

## Don't disable it

A FAB has no disabled style. If its action isn't available, leave the FAB out rather than disabling it, as M3 asks.

## In a NavRail

In a NavRail's `fab` slot, the rail decides whether the FAB is extended, as M3 specifies: extended while the rail is expanded, and a plain FAB while it's collapsed, whatever `isExtended` says. It follows the rail's width, using a container query on the rail, so it changes as the rail expands and collapses. Its tooltip follows too, showing only while the rail is collapsed and the label is hidden.

## Accessibility

The `label` is always the FAB's accessible name, whether or not it's visible. The tooltip repeats it for sighted users while it's hidden, so the tooltip is hidden from assistive tech. It can be dismissed with Escape, and stays open while the pointer is over it, as WCAG 1.4.13 requires.
