## Primary or secondary

- **Primary tabs** sit at the top of a content pane, under an app bar, for its main destinations.
- **Secondary tabs** sit within content, for a second level of hierarchy. When both are used, secondary tabs always go below primary ones.

## Fixed or scrollable

Fixed tabs share the row in equal widths. Keep to four or fewer: five or more get cramped, so use scrollable tabs instead.

- **On a phone,** fixed tabs fill the width.
- **From a medium window up,** each fixed tab is as wide as the widest one, and the group sits at the start, or in the center with `align="center"`. The divider under them still spans the full width.

Scrollable tabs (`isScrollable`) scroll sideways when they don't fit, for longer labels or more tabs. The first starts 52px from the leading edge, so the cut-off tab at the other end shows there's more. They don't loop back to the start: an endlessly scrolling set can trap screen reader users.

## Labels

Keep labels short. A fixed tab's label can wrap to a second line, after which it's cut off with an ellipsis. The full label stays the tab's accessible name. For longer labels, use scrollable tabs, whose labels never wrap.

## Icons

Use simple, widely recognized icons. Give every tab an icon, or none: don't mix tabs with and without icons in one set.

## Badges

A Tab's `badgeLabel` shows a count, or `""` for a dot. Update or remove a tab's badge once its content has been seen. A count shows up to 4 characters, so a number over 999 shows as `999+`.

## Placement

Tabs attached to an app bar move with it: don't let them scroll behind it. Whether the tabs stay fixed at the top of the page or scroll off and come back as the page scrolls up is up to the page.
