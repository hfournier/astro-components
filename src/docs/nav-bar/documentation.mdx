## Compact and medium

The bar is 64px high, on `surface-container`. Below the medium breakpoint (600px), its destinations are M3's vertical items: each takes an equal share of the bar's width, with its icon in a 56×32px active indicator and its label 4px below it. From medium up, they're M3's horizontal items, grouped in the middle of the bar: each has its icon and label side by side in a 40px-high indicator. The items follow the window's width rather than the bar's, since M3 bases the change on the window size class.

## Layout

The bar is `fixed` to the bottom of the viewport at `z-30`. It adds the device's bottom safe area (`env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`) below its 64px, so on a phone with a home indicator it sits above the indicator, with its background filling the space under it. The safe area is only non-zero with `viewport-fit=cover` in the page's viewport meta tag. Since the bar is out of the page flow, leave room for it under the page's content, for example with `pb-[calc(4rem+env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]` on the `<body>`.

M3 uses a navigation bar in compact windows and a navigation rail from medium up. For that pattern, put a NavBar and a NavRail on the same page, hide the bar from medium up with `class="medium:hidden"`, and hide the rail below medium with `class="max-medium:hidden"` (it then never opens as a modal). Both are labelled "Main", which is fine, since only one is shown at a time.

For a bar that isn't fixed, such as one inside a mockup, pass `class="static"`. The previews here do that, and give each bar its own `label`, so that they don't add more "Main" navigations to this page.
