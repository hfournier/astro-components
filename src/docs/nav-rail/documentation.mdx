## Collapsed and expanded

Collapsed, the rail is 96px wide, with each destination's icon in a pill above its label. Expanded, it's 220-360px wide (see `expandedWidth`), with 20px side padding; each destination is a row with its label beside its icon, the FAB is extended, and the `sub-items` slot shows. The rail animates between the two widths. Its content starts 44px from the top, M3's top space (20px below medium; see Small screens).

An expandable rail's MenuToggle expands and collapses it from medium up. As M3 allows, it starts expanded from the large breakpoint (1200px) up, and collapsed below it; the toggle is set before the rail is first painted, so the rail doesn't change width as the page loads. Within one window size, the rail stays as the user left it; crossing into or out of large, or medium, resets it to that size's default. It isn't kept from one page to the next.

The rail is the only thing whose state decides this: it's a size container named `rail`, and NavRailItem, Fab, NavRailSubItems and the rail's own content restyle themselves once it's wider than 160px (between the collapsed and expanded widths), with the `rail-expanded:` and `rail-collapsed:` variants defined in `theme.css`. A container's width can't depend on its content, so the expanded rail has a set width rather than growing to fit its labels; a label too long for it wraps between words, and its destination grows taller to hold it.

## Small screens

Below the medium breakpoint (600px), the rail is hidden off the start edge of the page (the left, or the right in a right-to-left page), all but its MenuToggle, which stays in place at the top of that edge of the viewport (20px from the top and the side, where it sits in the open rail), over the page. Every rail has the toggle below medium; one that isn't expandable has it only there. Pressing it slides the rail in underneath it, and the toggle shows the menu-open icon; pressing it again slides the rail back out. Since the toggle never moves, focus stays on it. Below medium it's filled with the rail's background, so it's readable over whatever is behind it while the rail is closed (a header on any `bgColor`, say), and blends into the rail when it's open.

The rail opens as M3's modal expanded rail, at its expanded width (see `expandedWidth`), on `surface-container` (or the rail's own `bgColor`, if you set one) at elevation 2, with large corners on its end side: a scrim covers the page, which is inert until the rail closes. Only the current page's section of sub-items shows, after the destinations. A rail that isn't expandable gets those sections from its NavDrawer, which moves them into the rail below medium and back from medium up. The rail also closes with Escape, which brings focus back to the toggle, or a click on the scrim.

The toggle sits over whatever is at the top of the page's start edge, so leave room for it there: it covers 20-76px from the top and the start edge, so a Header that starts the page could be at least 96px high (`max-medium:min-h-24`, centering the toggle in it) with its content starting at 84px (`max-medium:ps-21`). That's why the rail's content starts 20px from the top below medium, rather than M3's 44px: the toggle doubles as the page's menu button over its header while the rail is closed. It's `fixed` within the rail, which is why the rail slides by its `inset-inline-start` rather than a transform (a transform would make the toggle slide with it).

## Layout

From medium up, the rail is sticky, full height, and scrolls on its own when its content is taller than the viewport. Put it first in your layout, for example in a grid with `grid-cols-1 medium:grid-cols-[auto_1fr]` on its parent (with a NavDrawer after it, `large:grid-cols-[auto_auto_1fr]`); as the first grid column, it's on the right in a right-to-left page, as M3 asks. Below medium it's out of the layout, so the page needs only one column. It's `z-30`, so a NavDrawer slides out from under it. It renders a scrim after itself, which is only ever shown below medium. Its destinations are in a `<nav>` labelled "Main" (see `label`), so the rail should be the page's only main navigation.

There's no live preview here, since it would add a second main navigation to the page. This site's own navigation rail, on the start edge of every page, is the live example.
