## Standard and modal

A standard sheet sits beside the main content, and both can be used at the same time. Use one in medium and larger windows, for actions that affect the main content, such as filters, or for supplemental content and details. Put it in your layout, such as the last column of a grid, so the content beside it narrows to make room. Docked, it's sticky, as tall as the window, with a divider on its inner edge.

A modal sheet floats over the page, at elevation 1 with large corners on its inner edge, and the page under its scrim can't be used until it closes. Use one in compact windows, where there's no room beside the content, or for content the user deals with before going back to the page. A sheet can be modal in small windows and standard in larger ones by rendering one of each and showing whichever fits, as this site's table of contents does.

## Roles

M3 gives side sheets the dialog role. The modal sheet is a `<dialog>`, so it has it. The standard sheet is deliberately an `<aside>` (the complementary role) instead: on the web it's part of the page, used alongside the main content, and it doesn't take focus when it appears, so announcing it as a dialog would mislead.

## Placement

Put the sheet on the end edge, the default, unless there's a reason not to: navigation usually sits on the start edge, and the two would clash. Detach it (`isDetached`) if you want it to float; M3 detaches it by 16px, and never more.

## Close and back buttons

Every sheet has a close button, since M3 requires one: without it, people can't predict how the sheet opens and closes, or tell whether it's temporary. Leave it out (`showCloseX={false}`) only when the sheet is a permanent part of the layout that can't be closed.

Add a back button (`showBack`) when the sheet has views of its own to move back through, such as an item's details opened from a list in the sheet. Listen for its `back` event and show the previous view. Both buttons show their label in a tooltip; set `closeLabel` and `backLabel` on pages in other languages.

## Content

Side sheets never scroll sideways, so keep content within the sheet's width: let text wrap, give flex children `min-w-0` so they can shrink, and make tables and media responsive (e.g. `max-w-full` on images). Anything wider is cut off.

Use dividers to separate the actions from the content, as the actions row does, and to separate user-generated content from system-generated content.

## Actions

Show the main action with the most emphasis, such as a filled or tonal Save before a text Cancel.

## Layout

Following M3, the sheet has 24px of padding at its start and end (16px at the start of the header with a back button), 12px between the header's elements, and its actions in a 72px row at the bottom (16px above the buttons, 24px below), aligned to the start. Only the content scrolls; the headline and actions stay in place.
