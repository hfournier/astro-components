## Standard and modal

A standard sheet sits beside the main content, and both can be used at the same time. Put it in your layout, such as the last column of a grid, so the content beside it narrows to make room. Docked, it's sticky, as tall as the window, with a divider on its inner edge.

A modal sheet floats over the page, at elevation 1 with large corners on its inner edge, and the page under its scrim can't be used until it closes. Use one on smaller screens, where there's no room beside the content, or for content the user deals with before going back to the page. A sheet can be standard on larger screens and modal on smaller ones by rendering one of each and showing whichever fits, as this site's table of contents does.

## Layout

Following M3, the sheet has 24px of padding at its start and end, 12px between the headline and the close button, and its actions in a 72px row at the bottom (16px above the buttons, 24px below), aligned to the start. Only the content scrolls; the headline and actions stay in place.
