# The docked toolbar keeps every control a tab stop, lays out by window size, and re-points tonal toggles

M3 Expressive's docked toolbar replaces the bottom app bar: a full-width bar of page actions at the bottom of the window (#211). Three things about it weren't settled by earlier components. We decided:

1. **Tab and the arrow keys both move between its controls.** M3's Accessibility page gives "Tab or Arrows" for moving between them and says Tab moves through all of them, so every control stays a tab stop, as in ButtonGroup. WAI-ARIA's toolbar pattern, one tab stop with a roving tabindex, would contradict M3's Tab behaviour, and a toolbar can hold controls a roving tabindex can't hold, such as a text field. The left and right arrows move to the previous or next control Tab would reach, mirrored right to left, stopping at the ends and skipping disabled ones. A control that uses the arrow keys itself keeps them: a text input, textarea, select, slider, spin button, combobox, radio, or editable content. The script listens on the `window`, so it runs after the document listeners of a ButtonGroup or ChipGroup inside the toolbar. A group that moved focus has called `preventDefault()`, and the toolbar leaves the key alone; at its ends, a group doesn't, and the toolbar moves on to the next control. Keys inside a popover or dialog opened from the toolbar, such as a menu, are left to it.
2. **Its items spread evenly in a compact window and are centered from medium.** M3's Specs give a 16dp leading and trailing space and a spacing between items from 4dp to 32dp, and its Guidelines space items evenly on phones and center them from medium windows. CSS can't cap `space-between` at 32px without knowing the items' widths, so the toolbar switches at M3's `medium` breakpoint (ADR-0014): `justify-between` with a 4px gap below it, `justify-center` with a 32px gap from it, which is Compose's fixed arrangement. Each item sits in a grid cell at least 48px wide, its touch target, as Compose lays out an icon button, so the spacing is between targets. A page can override both with classes.
3. **A tonal toggle icon button takes the toolbar's selected-button colors through role tokens.** M3's Specs give a toolbar button's container and a selected button's container and icon: surface container, then secondary container and on-secondary-container, in a standard toolbar; primary container, then surface container and on-surface, in a vibrant one. The selected button is M3's toggle tonal icon button, so ButtonIcon's tonal toggle now reads four role tokens, `--theme-color-tonal-toggle`, `--theme-color-on-tonal-toggle` and their `-selected` pair, defined in `theme.css` on its usual secondary roles. ToolbarDocked re-points them, as BaseWrapper re-points `--theme-color-on-bg-variant` per background. Its standard icon buttons get their icon color that way already: BaseWrapper's surface container and primary container give on-surface-variant and on-primary-container.

We rejected styling the toggles from the toolbar with descendant selectors, such as `[&_[aria-pressed=true]]:bg-…`. They tie with ButtonIcon's own `aria-pressed:` classes on specificity, so which one wins depends on Tailwind's class order. We also rejected computing the spacing in the script: the layout would shift once the script ran, and the toolbar would need a resize observer for a value two breakpoints describe.

## Consequences

- Other buttons don't take the toolbar's selected colors yet: a labelled toggle Button or a standard toggle icon button keeps its own. M3's Specs give the label text the same roles as the icon, so a toggle Button could read the same tokens later.
- The floating toolbar (#212) can reuse the arrow-key behaviour and the role tokens, with its own container colors.
- M3 lets a docked toolbar animate off screen as the page scrolls, and Compose has an exit-always scroll behaviour for it. ToolbarDocked stays on screen; hiding on scroll is left for later.
