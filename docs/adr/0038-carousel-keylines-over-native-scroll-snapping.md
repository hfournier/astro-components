# The carousel runs Compose's keylines over a natively scrolling, snapping row

M3 Expressive's carousel shows a row of visual items that change size and shape as they move through it, with a parallax effect on their visuals, and snaps them into place (#213, the multi-browse layout). M3's Specs give the measurements (16dp leading and trailing padding, 8dp above and below and between items, small items 40 to 56dp, 28dp corners) but not how items are sized as they move. Compose's carousel does that with keylines: places in the row where an item has a set size. The other layouts (#214, #215) reuse the same model. We decided:

1. **Compose's carousel layout is ported to `src/carousel.ts`, pure functions checked against Compose's own unit tests.** That's its arrangement (how many large, medium and small items fit near the preferred width), keyline lists, strategy (the keylines shifting step by step near each end, so the first and last items can become large and the content padding shows), snap positions, and `Modifier.carouselItem`'s placement of each item. `CarouselLayout.spec.ts` runs the cases from Compose's `MultiBrowseTest`, `StrategyTest` and `KeylineSnapPositionTest`, outside a browser, as `src/shapes.ts` is shared by a component and its spec.
2. **The row scrolls and snaps natively, with every item laid out at the large size.** As in Compose, where the carousel is a pager whose pages are the large size, the `<ul>` holds `<li>`s at the large width, 8dp apart, so the browser's scroll position is the strategy's scroll offset (its absolute value right to left). The browser handles scrolling with a pointer, touch and keyboard. `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory` with `scroll-snap-align: start` snaps them, and the script gives each `<li>` a `scroll-margin-inline-start` of Compose's snap offset for it, so an item snaps where Compose's would. The snapping isn't limited to one item per swipe. Compose's default fling for multi-browse is one item at a time, but M3's Overview says multi-browse carousels "scroll through many items at once".
3. **Each item's visible box is a positioned link or button inside its `<li>`, sized and placed per frame. Its visual stays at the large size.** On scroll, once a frame, the script computes each item's keyline size and translation and sets them as custom properties on its `<li>`. The `<a>` or `<button>` is absolutely positioned at that size, centered on the `<li>` and moved by the translation, with `inset-inline-start` so it mirrors right to left. Its visual, inside it, stays at the large size, centered where the `<li>` is. So the item's edges move over the visual as it shrinks and grows, which is the parallax, as Compose's mask over a large-size item gives it. Resizing the item box rather than clipping it with `clip-path` keeps its focus ring, hover shadow and state layer drawn by plain CSS. Each item moves toward the row's view, never beyond its scroll extent, so the scroll width doesn't change as items move.
4. **The carousel is a region described as a carousel, holding a list, and each item is a link or a button.** M3's Accessibility page asks that the container be labelled and announce the number of items and the focused one, that Tab land on the first item rather than the container, that Tab or the arrow keys move between items, and that the up and down arrow keys leave the carousel. A `role="region"` with `aria-roledescription="carousel"` is the WAI-ARIA carousel pattern's container. A `<ul>` of `<li>`s gives screen readers the count and position. The row is `tabindex="-1"`, so Firefox doesn't make the scrolling `<ul>` a tab stop. The arrow keys reuse `moveFocusWithArrows` (ADR-0036). Up and down move focus to the control before or after the carousel, found with `tabbableIn`, which `moveFocusWithArrows` now shares. An item that takes focus or is clicked while it isn't large scrolls to its snap position, so it's seen in full; clicked too, as Safari doesn't focus a clicked link or button. Each `<li>` stays at the large width and takes no pointer events, as a small item moves over the next one's `<li>`. A CarouselItem is a link (`href`) or a button (`as="button"`), like Card's interactive styles (ADR-0030). A name is required on the carousel, and an `href` on a link item, or the build fails (ADR-0021).
5. **Item text is a `label` slot, light on a scrim, at the item's start, fading as the item shrinks.** M3's Guidelines show brief white text at the bottom start of an item's visual, which adapts to the item's size, but its tokens don't style it. The text is Title Medium, `white`, over a gradient of the `scrim` role, so it meets contrast over any visual. It's laid out at the large width, so it doesn't rewrap as the item shrinks, and fades out over the first 80dp the item is masked, as MDC's carousel documentation does in its `onMaskChanged` example.
6. **Reduced motion keeps every item large, and the row natively padded.** M3's Accessibility page: with reduced motion, the parallax is removed, items no longer expand as they come into view, and all are the same size, reaching the window's edges. The script clears the keylines, and the row keeps its 16dp padding and snapping. The same holds without the script.

We rejected CSS scroll-driven animations for the sizes. Firefox doesn't ship them, and every item's keyframes would depend on the row's width and item count, which the script recomputes anyway. We rejected a scrollbar or arrow buttons, which M3 asks not to put beside or over the carousel; M3's "Show all" button below it is the docs' answer for reaching every item.

## Consequences

- At the start and end, Compose takes the 16dp content padding out of every visible item's size, which takes a small item there to about 35dp, under the Specs' 40 to 56dp. The port follows the Specs instead: the padding comes out of the items larger than the smallest only, so the small item keeps its arranged size. This is the one place it departs from Compose's layout.
- The script sets custom properties on every item each frame while the row scrolls. That's fine for M3's suggested ten or so items, but a carousel of hundreds would do work for items far out of view.
- Hero and center-aligned hero (#214) add Compose's `heroKeylineList`, and uncontained (#215) `uncontainedKeylineList`. Each is a new arrangement in `src/carousel.ts` behind a `layout` prop, with the same row, items and placement.
- WebKit's Tab skips links by default, as Safari's does, so link items are reached with Option-Tab there unless the "Press Tab to highlight each item" setting is on.
