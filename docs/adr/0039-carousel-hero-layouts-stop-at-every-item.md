# The hero carousels are Compose's hero keylines behind a `layout` prop, stopping at every item

M3 Expressive's hero layout shows one large item and a small one after it, and its center-aligned hero centers the large item between two small ones; both swipe "through one item at a time" (#214). ADR-0038 built the carousel's row, items and placement for the multi-browse layout, and left the other layouts as new arrangements behind a `layout` prop. We decided:

1. **`layout` takes `"multi-browse"`, `"hero"` and `"center-aligned-hero"`, and `src/carousel.ts` ports Compose's `heroKeylineList` as `arrangeHero`.** That's its arrangement (one small item, or two when centered, none for a single item or where a large item 25% larger than the small ones wouldn't fit), its start-aligned and center-aligned keyline lists, and its fallback to start-aligned for fewer than three items. `CarouselLayout.spec.ts` runs the cases of Compose's `CenteredHeroTest`, and the center-aligned cases of `KeylineSnapPositionTest`, which the existing strategy and snap code pass unchanged. Compose has no public start-aligned hero carousel; `heroKeylineList` with `isCentered = false` is the one M3's Specs draw. The script picks the arrangement from `data-ac-carousel`, which now holds the layout, and the rest of ADR-0038 holds as it is.
2. **A hero carousel's large item fills the carousel, but for its small items, unless it has a `preferredItemWidth`.** This is Compose's default (`Dp.Unspecified`). With a width, a wide carousel fits more large items, as M3's Guidelines ask for larger screens. Multi-browse keeps its 186px default. Before the script runs, a hero carousel's items are the row's width less room for their small items, through a `calc()` on `--ac-carousel-item-size`.
3. **One item at a time is `scroll-snap-stop: always` on every item.** Compose gets it from `singleAdvanceFlingBehavior`, which limits a fling to the next page. On the web, a snap stop the row can't scroll past does the same for a swipe, a trackpad fling and the keyboard, natively. Multi-browse keeps `normal`, as ADR-0038 has it.
4. **With reduced motion, a hero carousel's items keep the arranged large size, and a center-aligned one snaps them to the center.** M3's Accessibility page: for hero carousels with reduced motion, "the small carousel item is only partially shown on screen". The script still works out the keylines, takes their large size for every item, and clears the rest. Without the keylines, a center-aligned hero's items take `scroll-snap-align: center`, so the item that has focus is centered with a part of each neighbour beside it, and the row's ends still start and end at its 16px padding.

We rejected a separate component per layout. They share the row, the items, the keyboard and the build checks, and differ only in their keylines and snap stop, as Compose's `HorizontalMultiBrowseCarousel` and `HorizontalCenteredHeroCarousel` share its internal `Carousel`. We rejected a script that limits each swipe to one item, which would fight the browser's own scrolling; `scroll-snap-stop` is supported in every engine.

## Consequences

- At rest, a center-aligned hero starts with its first item large at the row's start and the next two small after it, as Compose's documentation draws it ("item 1 selected: [1][2][3]"); it shows a small item, a centered large one and a small one from the second item on. M3's Specs draw the centered arrangement only, but a centered first item would leave its leading small place empty, so we follow Compose.
- As in multi-browse, the trailing small item reaches the container's edge at rest, and the 16px trailing padding shows once the row is scrolled to its end, as Compose's content padding does.
- At rest in a 412px carousel, a hero carousel's small item is 40px: Compose's arrangement shrinks it to the minimum to keep the large item nearest the carousel's width. With reduced motion, the next item shows about 24px past the padding.
- A swipe over a hero carousel stops at the next item even when it's flicked hard, which is M3's behavior but means a long carousel takes many swipes; the Show all button (ADR-0038) is still the way to reach every item.
- Without the script, or with reduced motion, a center-aligned hero's items snap to the center whatever their count, where the keylines would start-align fewer than three. With one or two items, the row can barely scroll, so they stay near its ends anyway.
