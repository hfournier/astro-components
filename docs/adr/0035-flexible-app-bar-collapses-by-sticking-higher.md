# A flexible app bar collapses by sticking higher, its top row pinned where the page pinned it

M3 Expressive's medium flexible and large flexible app bars put the small app bar's row of buttons on top and the headline and subtitle, larger, in a row of their own below. Scrolled, they "transform into small app bars" and "remain small until the page is scrolled back to the top" (#210). Compose does this with `TwoRowsTopAppBar` and an exit-until-collapsed scroll behavior: the bar takes the scroll first, its bottom row shrinking under the top row until only the top row is left, then lets the content scroll. A web page has no nested scrolling to take the scroll first. We decided:

1. **The pinned element sticks higher by the height of the headline's row.** The page pins a flexible bar as it pins a small one (ADR-0034): `sticky top-0` on the header, or on the bar itself in a scrolling box. The script reads that `top` once, then sets the pinned element's `top` to it minus the bar's height less its 64px top row. The headline's row then scrolls away with the content, as Compose's bottom row does, and the content moves with the scroll the whole time, with no jump. The script measures the bar each time it runs, so a headline that wraps to another line moves it up further.
2. **The top row sticks, inside the bar, where the page pinned the bar.** It's `sticky` too, at `--ac-app-bar-pin-top`, which the script sets to the page's `top`. It's opaque, on the bar's background and above the headline's row, so that row passes under it. Until the script sets the variable, the row's `top` is `auto`, so a bar that isn't pinned doesn't move its top row as the page scrolls.
3. **Collapsed is pinned and scrolled.** With the headline's row gone, the pinned element is at its `top` exactly when ADR-0034 marks the bar scrolled. Pinned with `sticky`, a flexible bar is then marked `data-ac-app-bar-collapsed` too. The small app bar's headline and subtitle in the top row fade in on fast effects, as the bar fills. They repeat the large ones, which stay in the accessibility tree, so they're `aria-hidden`.
4. **The bar's least height is M3's, the text at its bottom.** M3's tokens give the heights (112dp and 136dp with a subtitle; 120dp and 152dp) and its measurements 12dp below the text. The bar takes the height as a `min-height`, and the text sits at its bottom, so a headline that wraps grows the bar instead of being cut short.

We rejected collapsing the bar's height when the content scrolls (a script switching the bar between expanded and collapsed, the height transitioning). The bar's height is in the page's layout, so the content would jump up by the headline row's height on the first scroll. Scroll anchoring would then move the scroll back toward the top and expand the bar again, and close to the top it could flicker between the two. We also rejected scroll-driven animations, which Firefox doesn't ship yet, and `scroll-state()` container queries, which are Chromium only (ADR-0034). Setting `top` on the page's element is the one thing the bar does outside itself. It's limited to the pinned element ADR-0034's script already finds.

## Consequences

- A flexible bar pinned with `fixed` fills but doesn't collapse: a fixed element doesn't scroll, so its headline's row has nowhere to go.
- The page's `top` is read once per pinned element. A page that changes it later, such as at a breakpoint, needs the inline `top` removed so the script reads it again.
- Compose fades the large headline out and the small one in as the bar collapses. Here, the large one passes under the top row and the small one fades in once it's gone.
- A bar added to the page after it loaded collapses from the next scroll on, like ADR-0034's fill.
