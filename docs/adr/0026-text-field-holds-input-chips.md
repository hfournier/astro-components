# A text field holds input chips in a chip set around its input

> **Partly superseded by ADR-0032**: §1 and the rejected "chip field" alternative. A field of chips is now TextFieldChips, a public component over the BaseTextField it shares with TextField, and `chipOverflow` is its `overflow`. The other decisions stand, in TextFieldChips.

M3's Guidelines page puts input chips "inline with the cursor in a text field", as for contacts in an email's "To" field (#251). Text typed there becomes a chip. Backspace with the cursor before a chip selects it, and a second Backspace deletes it. Chips wrap onto new rows, or scroll sideways in a field one row high, the overflow following text: an unfocused field shows its start, and focusing it puts the cursor at the end. Chips can be reordered in a field and moved into another. ChipInput (#201, ADR-0025) left all of this to the field and said the page keeps a chip's data, since the chip doesn't submit. Neither M3 nor Compose defines a field of chips. Compose's `InputChip` sits in whatever row the app builds.

We decided:

1. **TextField takes the chips in a `chips` slot.** The field keeps everything it already draws: the filled and outlined containers, the label, icons, supporting text, error state and counter. It gains `chipOverflow` (`wrap` or `scroll`, ChipGroup's two overflows) and no other prop. A textarea with chips, `chipOverflow` without chips, or chips in a field with no name for their group, fails the build (ADR-0021).
2. **The chips and the input share one ChipGroup**, so they wrap and scroll as one line of content, the input taking the rest of the last row, and the group's arrow keys move between the chips. An internal component, `TextFieldChips`, renders it, with the chip-field script, so a page with plain text fields doesn't load that script. The group is named by the field's label.
3. **TextField's styles follow its own input, marked `data-ac-text-field-part="input"`,** not any `input` inside it, since a chip's edit field and the chips' hidden values are inputs too. The field shows its focus indicator while a chip in it is focused, and its label floats while it holds a chip, as the label would over text.
4. **Committed text becomes an editable chip.** Enter commits the text. The input fires a cancelable `add`, with the text as `detail.value` and `detail.label`, which the page can refuse, leaving the text in the field, or relabel, such as a contact's name for an address it submits as the value. A new chip copies a ChipInput rendered with `isEditable` in a `<template>`, as M3 shows chips converted from text being editable. Enter on an empty field still submits its form.
5. **The chips submit, the text doesn't.** The field's `name` goes on a hidden input per chip, holding its body's `value` attribute or else its label, in the chips' order, whether or not a chip is selected. A mutation observer keeps them in step with the chips. A `required` field's input is required only while it has no chips.
6. **Moving chips: the pointer, and Shift with an arrow key.** M3 gives no keys for this. Shift with ArrowLeft or ArrowRight (mirrored right to left) moves a chip a place within its field. Shift with ArrowUp or ArrowDown moves it to the end of the previous or next chip field in the same form. Unmodified arrow keys stay ChipGroup's, and Alt+ArrowLeft is the browser's Back. A polite status says where the chip went ("Blake, 2 of 2 in Cc"). Dragging is done with pointer events rather than HTML drag and drop, which Firefox doesn't start from a `<button>`: a mouse or pen carries the chip, in BaseChip's dragged state, the chips around it make room, and Escape puts it back. Touch scrolls, keeping the keyboard as the alternative. Chips that change place slide on the fast spatial spring, or move at once under reduced motion.

We rejected a separate pattern component (a "chip field"), which would duplicate TextField's container, label and states or wrap TextField from outside, where it can't place chips inline with the input. We also rejected native drag and drop (§6), and Alt or Ctrl with the arrow keys, which browsers and operating systems claim for Back and for switching desktops.

## Consequences

- ChipGroup now ignores arrow keys with a modifier, leaving them to whatever handles them, such as moving a chip.
- A chip is like an `<option>`: it shows its label and stands for its body's `value`, or its label when it has none. So ChipInput's edit opens on the value, such as a contact's address rather than their name, and the text committed becomes both, the `value` attribute dropped when they're the same. Editing a shown name while the address stays would change nothing the form submits. M3's Guidelines show a contact opening as "Fabian Reza <rezzian120@gmail.com>": a page does that with ChipInput's cancelable `edit` event, whose `detail.text` it can rewrite, and splits the text back on `change`, whose `detail.label` and `detail.value` it can set, as on `add`. This stays the page's job rather than an email chip's, since M3 has only the generic input chip, and only the page knows what its chips stand for.
- A filled field of chips with a label is 64px tall for one row: the floated label's 24px above a 32px chip, and 8px below. Unlabelled or outlined, it's M3's 56px. Each wrapped row adds ChipGroup's 16px gap and a 32px chip.
- A form's reset doesn't bring back removed chips.
